\begin{abstract}
We study a solver architecture for real linear equality systems
\(Ax=b\), \(A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\), \(b\in\mathbb{R}^m\), without
assuming consistency, full rank, or a unique solution. The state is an affine
bundle in canonical coordinates \((P,x)\), where \(P\) projects onto the
accepted normal space and \(x\) is the minimum-norm point satisfying the
accepted constraints. For an incoming equality \(a^Tz=\beta\), the quantities
\(g=(I-P)a\) and \(\rho=\beta-a^Tx\) give an exact insertion calculus:
nonzero \(g\) grows the bundle by one normal direction, while zero \(g\)
separates redundancy from contradiction.

In finite precision the router separates operational classification, numerical rank and candidate quality. It uses guarded direct solves, prefix bundles, tangent probes, compressed correction and source checks. We derive a sufficient condition for source-rank promotion when formation and factorization errors have verified upper bounds. The executable rank interval also reflects policy thresholds.

Three proof objects construct nearby real systems with exact uniqueness, nonuniqueness or inconsistency. If the checker bounds the row perturbations outward, each construction yields an upper bound on the corresponding status-set distance. The audit operation attempts all three types independently of the router. A finite-input overflow counterexample to the infinite-status checker gives an accepted zero radius at strictly positive distance. Thus the mathematical theorem requires an arithmetic premise that the released binary can violate.

Two property batteries, of 8{,}335 and 2{,}271 checks, report no hard
failure. Exact reconstruction of translated inputs identifies 27 diagnostic cases in which rounding the right-hand side changed the exact compatibility of the stored problem. The experiments support a restricted claim of practical viability
for dense equality classification, not universal superiority over
general-purpose numerical linear algebra.
\end{abstract}
